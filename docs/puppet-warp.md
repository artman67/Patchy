# Puppet Warp

Implementation, UI, and patent contract for Edit > Puppet Warp. The binding rule is the Puppet Warp bullet in [legal-constraints.md](legal-constraints.md); the claim summary below is the clearance record behind it (claim check of 2026-10-07, public claim text, not legal advice).

## Product and UI contract

- **Entry.** Edit > Puppet Warp sits above Free Transform (Photoshop's position): `editPuppetWarpAction`, hotkey id `edit.puppet_warp`, no default key, blocked in channel view. `MainWindow::puppet_warp_active_layer` applies Liquify's guards: an 8-bit RGB pixel layer, Smart Objects refused ("Rasterize the Smart Object before using Puppet Warp"; no native Puppet Warp Smart Filter is authored), text and shape layers offered Rasterize, pixel lock, Quick Mask, and document-channel views refused. The canvas session lives in `src/ui/canvas_widget_puppet_warp.cpp`, the solver in `src/core/puppet_warp.{hpp,cpp}`.
- **Canvas.** A click on the mesh adds a pin and selects it; press-drag adds one and drags it. Dragging a pin moves every selected pin. Shift-click toggles a pin in the selection, Alt-click removes a pin, Delete or Backspace removes the selected pins, a click off the mesh deselects. Pins are yellow discs with a dark center while selected. Nothing else is drawn around a pin.
- **Options bar** (the session owns the row; object names `puppetWarp*`): Mode (Rigid, Normal, Distort; default Normal), Density (Fewer Points, Normal, More Points; default Normal), Expansion (-100 to 100 px, default 2), Show Mesh (on), Pin Depth + and - (selected pins' depth up or down one step), Rotate (Auto or Fixed for the selected pins) with an angle field (editable only for Fixed; under Auto it shows the solution's own rotation at the first selected pin), Remove all pins, then the shared apply and cancel buttons. The free transform/warp toggle is hidden. Values persist for the app run in the widgets, not in settings.
- **Ending the session.** Enter or apply commits; Esc or cancel discards; a tool, layer, or channel switch commits (Warp Transform's rule). Undo and Redo inside the session step through pin edits only (Photoshop); the document history changes only on commit. A commit is one resample of the original pixels and one undo entry, "Puppet Warp". With no pin moved, no nonzero Fixed angle, and Expansion at 0 or more, commit is a no-op (no undo entry). A negative Expansion trims the layer to the shrunken mesh, as previewed. The whole layer warps (the selection is ignored), a layer mask is left unchanged (as Warp Transform), and the result is RGBA.

## Solver and renderer

- **Mesh** (`build_puppet_warp_mesh`): a regular lattice over the layer bounds grown by the positive Expansion. Spacing is the longest layer side divided by 24, 48, or 96 (Fewer, Normal, More), rounded up, at least 2 px. A cell belongs to the mesh when the alpha > 0 mask, dilated (positive) or eroded (negative) by Expansion with a square structuring element, touches it (summed-area tables). `handle_radius` is twice the spacing.
- **Deformation** (`deform_puppet_warp_mesh`): every mesh cell splits so render cells are at most 4 px, and every node gets its own closed-form moving-least-squares solve (Schaefer, McPhail and Warren 2006) with weights 1/|p - v|^2. Rigid = rigid MLS; Normal = the mean of the rigid and similarity matrices; Distort = affine MLS, falling back to similarity with fewer than three non-collinear constraints. One effective constraint is a pure translation; a node on a constraint maps exactly to its target. There is no triangle mesh, no global solve, and no iteration.
- **Fixed rotation** adds four constraints at plus and minus `handle_radius` on both axes around the pin, turned clockwise by the angle: two short crossing segments sampled at their ends, standing in for Schaefer's line-segment handles while keeping the point solve.
- **Pin depth**: nodes interpolate pin depth with the same weights. When depths differ, `WarpSurfaceGrid::cell_order` lists the mesh cells front-most first (stable, row-major ties). `resample_warped_rgba8` then renders in that order: the first opaque sample wins and fully clear samples never claim a pixel, so a limb's transparent margin cannot punch a hole in what lies behind it. An empty `cell_order` keeps the renderer's historical row-major, first-writer-wins behavior for every other caller.
- **Rendering**: the existing `resample_warped_rgba8`, inverting each cell per output pixel with the exact closed-form `invert_bilinear_cell`. Preview and commit render the same grid; the preview reuses Warp Transform's layer-hidden base cache and bounded patches (`build_warp_base_cache`, `set_warp_preview_patches`). A pin added on an already deformed mesh takes its rest position from the exact inverse of the front-most cell under the click (`puppet_warp_rest_point`), so adding it leaves the shape in place.
- Plain doubles in a fixed order, no randomness, no iteration: results are deterministic.

## Differences from Photoshop

- Photoshop solves a triangle mesh as rigidly as possible; Patchy solves each grid node with MLS (patent boundary below). Rigid resists stretching less than Photoshop's solver, and weights are straight-line distances, so a pin can pull a nearby limb that is not connected to it. The mesh is drawn as a grid, not triangles.
- Photoshop also rotates a pin by Alt-dragging a circle around it. Patchy rotates pins only through Rotate Fixed and the angle field, and has no menu next to a pin (patent boundary).
- Photoshop applies Puppet Warp to Smart Objects as an editable Smart Filter with stored pins. Patchy refuses Smart Objects (like Liquify) and pins live only for the session; nothing Puppet-Warp-specific is written to PSD.
- Density spacing and the Expansion range are Patchy's own values; Photoshop's internal numbers were not measured.

## Patent boundary (2026-10-07 claim check)

Verdict: clear with limits. Moving least squares on a grid is clear now; the Photoshop-style triangle solver is blocked.

- **University of Tokyo [US 8063917](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8063917B2/en)** (Igarashi's SIGGRAPH 2005 as-rigid-as-possible solver). Every independent claim needs an original triangle mesh with fixed and movable handles, a first step solving an intermediate mesh that allows per-triangle rotation, translation and uniform scale, a fitting step that rigidly fits each original triangle to it, and a final solve over triangle edge vectors. Expires about 2027-08-07 (371 date 2009-02-17 plus 495 days of term adjustment). Its fee status was not read, so it is treated as active. JP 4613313 and EP 1865467 are past their maximum term. Patchy has no triangle-mesh solve: each node is solved alone, so the similarity step, the per-triangle fit and the edge-vector solve are all absent. A per-triangle local/global ARAP started from a similarity solve is not cleared (single-element miss with equivalents exposure). GIMP's N-Point Deformation is GPL and the same method: never port it.
- **Adobe [US 9053553](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9053553B2/en)** (to 2033-01-25; its 11.5-year fee is due by 2026-12-09) and **[US 9454797](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9454797B2/en)** (to 2030-02-26). Every independent claim needs a pop-up menu displayed next to the selected control point that takes translation, rotation and depth values; US 9053553 also needs iterative optimization. Patchy keeps rotation and depth in the options bar and does not iterate.
- **Adobe [US 10643365](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10643365B1/en) and [US 10964081](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10964081B2/en)** (to 2038-11-20): a bend-handle control whose stationary portion surrounds the pin and sets a value while a movable portion follows the drag. Patchy draws nothing around a pin.
- **Adobe [US 9865073](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9865073B2/en), [US 10078910](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10078910B2/en), [US 10217262](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10217262B2/en)** (to 2035 and 2036): bounded biharmonic weights in every independent claim. Patchy uses inverse-distance MLS weights.
- **Adobe [US 10672174](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10672174B2/en)** (to 2038-06-28): a neural network that places pins. Patchy pins are only placed by the user.
- **Adobe [US 12482056](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12482056B2/en)** (about 2044-08-01): an energy with a thresholded regularization term. Not practiced.
- Judged not to read: Adobe US 10410317 (spline handles), US 10832446 (vector bone handles), US 10140764 (multiple input meshes), US 10510186 (vector Bezier continuity), US 11164355 (vector sculpting), US 11682149 (image-derived edge handle), US 8229247 (symmetry-driven editing, status unverified), US 12586148 (row shifting).
- Expired or lapsed: US 7952595 (TU Munich, per-region depth), US 8400472 (Technion), and Adobe's brush-warp family recorded in [liquify.md](liquify.md).
- No active US patent on 2D MLS image deformation was found (only CN104143204A, no US family seen).
- **Renderer**: keep `invert_bilinear_cell`'s exact inverse. Never switch to the four-triangle-area ratio inverse (Marvell [US 10055812](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10055812B1/en)).
- Prior art: Igarashi, Moscovich and Hughes (SIGGRAPH 2005); Schaefer, McPhail and Warren, "Image Deformation Using Moving Least Squares" (SIGGRAPH 2006); After Effects CS3 Puppet tool with overlap (depth) pins (2007); Zhu and Gortler 2007; Sorkine and Alexa 2007; GIMP Cage Transform (2.8, 2012); GIMP N-Point Deformation (2013, Playground 2018); Krita Cage; Photoshop CS5 Puppet Warp (April 2010).
- Limits of the check: US 8063917's fee status was not read (if it lapsed, the triangle-solver block ends now); statuses of US 8229247 and US 10055812 come from front pages only; the sweep is strong for Adobe but thin for Apple, Google, Microsoft, Celsys (Clip Studio Paint 4.0 shipped a Puppet Warp in 2025) and Chinese or Korean families; prosecution histories were not read; the "Puppet Warp" name was not checked against Adobe's trademarks.
- **Needs a new review before adding**: a triangle-mesh ARAP solver (not before about 2027-08-07 or a confirmed lapse of US 8063917, and still within the UI limits), a pop-up or popover next to a pin (2030-02-26 and 2033-01-25), any ring, dial or bend handle around a pin (2038-11-20), bounded biharmonic weights (2035 and 2036), automatic or learned pin placement (2038), thresholded local energies (2044), skeleton, spline or bone handles, geodesic or inside-the-shape weights (a likely next step; Zhu and Gortler 2007 is prior art, but it needs its own short check), and authoring Photoshop's Puppet Warp Smart Filter descriptor.
